Every vehicle in a fleet carries a stack of documents with expiry dates: insurance, inspection certificates, licences, permits, and for cross-border trucks, regional insurance cover too. Every driver carries their own. Multiply that by twenty, fifty or two hundred vehicles and you have hundreds of dates to track.

Most fleets track them in a spreadsheet, a notebook, or one person's memory. That works until it doesn't.

## Where the cost hides

**The vehicle stops earning.** A truck stopped at the roadside or held back at the yard because a document has lapsed is a truck not making money. The loss is not the renewal fee. It is every trip that vehicle misses while the paperwork is sorted out.

**The claim that isn't paid.** If insurance has lapsed when an accident happens, the cost of the vehicle, the cargo and any third-party damage can land on the business. One lapse on one day can wipe out a year of margin on that vehicle.

**Penalties and impounding.** Operating with expired documents invites fines, and in some cases the vehicle can be held until the problem is fixed.

**The client who walks away.** Corporate clients increasingly ask transporters for proof of compliance before awarding contracts. A fleet that cannot produce current documents quickly looks risky, even if every vehicle is in order.

**The hours nobody counts.** Someone in the office spends time every week chasing dates, calling drivers and checking papers. That time is real cost, and it scales with the fleet.

## Why spreadsheets fail

A spreadsheet only works if someone opens it at the right moment. Common failure points:

- The one person who keeps it is on leave or leaves the company.
- New vehicles or drivers get added late, or not at all.
- Renewal dates are entered wrongly and nobody notices.
- The sheet shows an expiry date but nobody owns the renewal.

The problem is not the tool. It is that the process depends on someone remembering.

## What good looks like

A reliable system does four things.

1. **One register.** Every vehicle, every driver, every document and its expiry date in one place, with a scan of the document attached.
2. **Automatic alerts.** Reminders go out well before expiry, for example at 60, 30 and 7 days, to the person responsible, not just to a general inbox.
3. **Clear ownership.** Each renewal has a named owner and a status: not started, in progress, or renewed.
4. **A view for management.** A simple daily or weekly summary of what is expiring soon and what is overdue.

## A real example

We built exactly this for **Duman Autos**. Their system records the expiry date of each logistics and vehicle document, runs a check every day, and emails alerts before anything lapses. Nobody has to remember to open a spreadsheet; the system does the remembering.

It is a small, quiet system, and that is the point. The best compliance tool is the one that works in the background and only speaks up when something needs attention.

## Getting started

You don't need a large project to fix this. Start by listing every document type your fleet depends on and who is responsible for each. If that list already feels hard to build, that is a sign the risk is bigger than it looks.
